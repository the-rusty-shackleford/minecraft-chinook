# D-0001 — The Chinook: life-size, by script, origin between the rotors

Rusty asked (2026-10-06) for a Huey and a Chinook, dyeable, flying with their own up and down,
carrying a sling load; life-size (one block a metre). The plan gave the Chinook animals and two
chests through its rear ramp, four wheels, a centre hook and a fifteen-wide sprayer.

**Decision**:

- **Scale and shape.** A Boeing CH-47 at one block a metre, built in metres from published figures
  (rotors 18.29 across; 3.78 across the fuel pods; a cabin 9.30 by 2.29 by 1.98 with a full-width
  ramp), a measured airframe (15.98 long, 3.81 across the front wheels, 5.79 to the aft pylon's top)
  and the U.S. Army's public-domain 3-view, whose side view, scaled by the fuselage's length, puts
  the hubs 11.95 apart as published (11.94). Its front and top views are not drawn to the side view's
  scale, so the widths are the published ones. An overlay of the side view on a render at the same
  frame lands within a few pixels from nose to tail.
- **The look.** A box fuselage with chamfered corners on a rounded nose with dark chin windows, a
  glassed cockpit, the low forward pylon and the tall aft pylon with an engine on each flank, the
  shaft tunnel between them, portholes along both sides, the crew door forward on the right, fuel
  pods along the lower sides, four wheels on struts, and the rear ramp: its underside rising from
  the cabin floor to the tail, the flanks above it following its line. Army green from the factory
  (`#424a37`), dyed whole; blades, metal and tyres are not paint.
- **Its origin is on the ground midway between the rotors**, the hull's middle: it turns about its
  middle as a tandem does, and Vanilla Wheels' body box stands under the cabin. Rotorcraft's `hull`
  names five boxes, and the rotors' radii set its culling (Rotorcraft's D-0001).
- **The rotors** turn opposite ways at the same rate (`speed` 1 and -1), three blades each, the aft
  a metre higher and set 60 degrees apart from the forward, so the blades pass over each other.
- **Seats, cargo, ramp**: the pilot in the right-hand seat, a copilot, four rows of three facing
  forward (Vanilla Wheels' riders face the vehicle's way, so the Army's side-facing seats would sit
  them sideways on them); six grown animals or twelve young in the rear cabin and two chests, all up
  the ramp, which is Vanilla Wheels' door swung -0.78 radians about its foot to the ground.
- **The sound**: 38 beats of 88 ms (each rotor's three blades at 225 rpm) from a CC0 recording made
  aboard a CH-47, its note 0.60 to 1.03, heard 80 blocks off.

**Why**: as the Huey's D-0001 -- life-size is Rusty's call, published dimensions make the shape
facts, and a script keeps the model reviewable and regenerable -- and the origin between the
rotors is where a tandem turns.

**Alternatives rejected**: *side-facing troop seats* (Vanilla Wheels turns every rider to face the
way the vehicle does); *the origin under one rotor* (the other end would swing round it).

**Verification**: `ChinookGameTests` (6): the profiles, the recipe, the lift (no build without its
four wheels), a flown circuit landing at full condition with the rider unhurt, cows up the lowered
ramp and none through it shut, and the Sling Container carried and set down whole. Not yet seen:
the model in the game beyond the booth, Rusty's eye.
